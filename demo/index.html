price-axis

A chart axis is the small piece of a product where "it looks wrong" is the only feedback you get. This page runs the rules in SPEC.md live: change the range, watch the step, the decimals and the bounds fall out of it, and watch the eight post-conditions hold. Try to break it.

What am I looking at?

Before a price chart can draw anything it has to settle three questions: where the horizontal lines go, what numbers to print beside them, and how much room to leave above and below the series. Get any of them wrong and the only feedback you will ever get is that the chart looks wrong.

This page settles those questions live. Change the range on the left; everything on the right recomputes. The chart draws a synthetic series across the range you set, shades that range, and rules a line at each tick. Result is what fell out: the step between ticks, the decimals each label carries, the count, the bounds. Post-conditions re-checks the eight properties from SPEC.md on every keystroke; the same eight are asserted twenty thousand times over in each test suite.

data min / max
the low and the high of the series
max ticks
the budget for horizontal lines
padding
headroom above and below, as a fraction of the span
min decimals
a floor, for instruments always quoted to a fixed precision
max decimals
the ceiling the step has to stay renderable under
